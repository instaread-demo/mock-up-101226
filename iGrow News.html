AMPERA Files Regulatory Engagement Plan With U.S. NRC

iGrow Newsdesk · 7th October 2026 · 4 min read
Follow iGrow News on Google News

Key Takeaways

  • AMPERA has filed a Regulatory Engagement Plan with the U.S. Nuclear Regulatory Commission (NRC) as part of the pre-application stage of licensing.
  • The Palm Beach Gardens, Florida, company expects to submit a combined manufacturing and fuel fabrication facility license application by October 2027.
  • The plan covers licensing under 10 CFR Parts 40, 53, 70 and 71, spanning nuclear material activities, fuel fabrication, reactor core manufacturing and deployment of modules to multiple sites.
  • AMPERA’s design is a subcritical, thorium-fueled modular system that relies on an external neutron generator to start and sustain operation.
  • Michael Hamer, who has more than 25 years of nuclear licensing experience across six countries, has joined as Director of Licensing & Regulatory Affairs.

Advanced nuclear developer AMPERA has formally opened its licensing dialogue with U.S. regulators. The Palm Beach Gardens, Florida, company said on October 5, 2026, that it had submitted a Regulatory Engagement Plan to the U.S. Nuclear Regulatory Commission (NRC), a step that sits within the pre-application phase of the licensing process.

AMPERA said it expects to follow the plan with a combined license application for a manufacturing and fuel fabrication facility by October 2027.

What AMPERA Filed With the NRC

During this pre-application stage, AMPERA is giving the regulator detailed material on its design features, the methodologies behind them and the supporting analysis. The company said the aim is to speed up the eventual review and reduce the risk of delays once a formal application is filed.

The plan is written to fit the NRC’s Office of Advanced Reactors (OAR), a recently created unit charged with applying an integrated regulatory approach to the safe rollout of new reactor technologies.

Licensing Scope Under Four Federal Rule Sets

The engagement plan is integrated rather than piecemeal. It addresses reactor licensing under Title 10 of the Code of Federal Regulations, specifically 10 CFR Parts 40, 53, 70 and 71. Together these cover the handling of nuclear material, fabrication of fuel, manufacture of reactor cores and the shipment and deployment of modules to more than one site.

AMPERA said rules for advanced microreactors are still shifting, with some changes already made and others expected. Its plan is built to stay flexible so the company can choose the most effective and efficient route to commercial approval and deployment around the world.

“Through this process, we will share technical details with regulators, protect our intellectual property and bolster confidence in advanced nuclear technology,” said Brian Matthews, Founder and CEO at AMPERA. “We are looking forward to reviewing our plans with the NRC for AMPERA’s commercial subcritical, thorium-fueled, modular system.”

How the AMPERA Reactor Design Differs

AMPERA’s approach departs from the conventional light water reactors that make up most of the existing fleet. Its system runs on thorium-based fuel and operates in a subcritical state, meaning an external neutron generator is used to start the reaction and keep it going. The company said this adds another layer of control over operation and opens the door to different fuel cycle approaches.

In July 2026, AMPERA showed what it described as the first full-scale, 3D-printed nuclear reactor module. Its spherical core uses a monolithic gyroid structure printed in silicon carbide and is designed to run for up to 30 years without being refueled.

The company also signed a strategic partnership with Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory on Aug. 26 covering advanced nuclear fuel development.

Building Out the Licensing Team

“We are making great progress, continuing to strengthen our team and looking forward to continued engagement with regulators and stakeholders,” said Dr. April Smith, Licensing Strategist at AMPERA. “We are bringing differentiated technology to market. The NRC is willing to take on the challenge, and we are excited to work with them on this first-of-a-kind technology in the microreactor space.”

AMPERA Appoints Michael Hamer to Lead Licensing

Alongside the filing, AMPERA named Michael Hamer as Director of Licensing & Regulatory Affairs. Hamer came through the U.S. Navy Nuclear Program and has worked on licensing for nuclear power projects in the U.S., South Korea, the United Arab Emirates, the United Kingdom, Poland and Canada over more than 25 years. He also has more than 16 years of experience in applied health physics.

Hamer and Smith will lead the company’s licensing work as AMPERA develops its “PowerNOW. NuclearNEXT.” platform, which targets factory-built power systems for data centers, defense, industrial and maritime users.
