Premature grey hair and hair thinning: How much do your genes matter?

How do genetics, lifestyle, and health factors influence premature grey hair and thinning? Learn about available treatments and care options.
Written by: Tavishi Dogra

Our hair plays a major role in how we look and how we see ourselves, which is why conditions like premature greying and thinning hair can often affect our confidence. Genetics also plays a major role in when and how these changes occur, although lifestyle, stress, nutrition, and overall health can also affect hair. Genetics also influences when you start going grey, how quickly your hair thins, and the pattern of hair loss. But having a family history doesn’t mean those changes will happen at the same age or to the same extent. Understanding the importance of heredity can help people have realistic expectations and, more importantly, find the right treatment and hair care options instead of relying on

Genetic predispositions: Premature greying of hair

A reduction in melanin production causes most grey or white hair, as the cells that make pigment in the hair follicle become less active with age. This is a perfectly normal process, but some people get greys in their 20s. Dermatologists say this may be due to genetic predisposition, but other causes include nutritional deficiencies, certain health conditions, and other underlying factors. Therefore, someone with a family history of early-onset grey hair who notices unusually early or sudden changes should consult a doctor.

Currently, no known treatment can completely reverse genetically driven greying, although in some cases vitamin supplementation and treatment of underlying conditions may help. If premature greying is due to an underlying condition or nutritional deficiency, treating the cause may help slow or stop further greying. Dyes are a great way to fight grey hair, a sign of aging, and hide it.

Genetic predispositions: Thinning of hair

Genetics can also contribute to hair thinning and loss. Androgenetic alopecia (pattern baldness) has a strong hereditary component. Androgen hormones and genetic susceptibility drive this condition, causing progressive hair loss in characteristic patterns that may differ between men and women.

In women, the pattern is more often diffuse thinning over the central scalp, with a widened part and a relatively preserved frontal hairline. The proper course of treatment depends on the patient’s age, sex, the type and stage of alopecia, and the presence of other symptoms; therefore, consult a professional to determine the cause of hair thinning. Minoxidil is one established treatment option for pattern hair loss, while other treatments may be considered depending on the individual diagnosis, sex, and medical history. Meanwhile, a hair transplant can restore hair in areas affected by suitable types of hair loss; however, it cannot eliminate the genetic predisposition to baldness.

When genetics isn’t the only factor

Family history can be an important hint, but don’t assume premature greying or thinning hair is genetic. Changes in hair may also be due to nutritional deficiencies, thyroid and other hormonal conditions, certain medications, illness, smoking, stress, and other factors. So if your hair loss is sudden or more rapid than usual, you may need to see a doctor to find out why.

Several effective treatments can prevent hair loss and delay greying, but the best option depends on the underlying cause and your individual condition. Those with a genetic predisposition to balding or early greying should not assume genetics is the only factor. Knowing the family history, age, the pattern and speed of hair change, lifestyle, and general health can help to determine the underlying cause and appropriate treatment. After a proper evaluation by a qualified dermatologist, a personalized treatment plan can significantly improve the condition of one’s hair and overall appearance.

Related FAQs

How often should I wash my hair?

People with oily scalps should wash their hair at least once a day. In the case of chemically treated hair, wash it less frequently.

How should I use an anti-hair fall shampoo?

Apply a small amount of the shampoo on wet hair and gently massage it into the scalp. Then rinse thoroughly with water and follow with conditioner if needed.

Is chemical-free shampoo good for your hair?

Yes, chemical-free shampoo can be beneficial as it reduces exposure to harmful synthetic chemicals. It can deeply condition your hair, and reduce hair and protein loss during combing.

What to look for in a hair shampoo?

You must choose a shampoo that contains natural ingredients like fenugreek seeds, shikakai, amla, aloe vera and more. This formulation can help prevent premature greying, strengthen your scalp, and hair, treat dandruff, reduce hair fall and promote hair growth.
