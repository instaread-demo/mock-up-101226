Jury Sees CCTV Of Beau Lamarre-Condon Hours After Sydney Couple’s Deaths

By Marvin Valdez
Posted on October 8, 2026
(NSW Supreme Court)

The 31-year-old former police officer denies two counts of murder and is claiming self-defence. He is accused of shooting the couple with his police-issue pistol at Baird’s share house in Paddington, in Sydney’s east, on 19 February 2024.

The Crown says the footage shows Lamarre-Condon picking up a hire van and returning to the Brown Street terrace that night, after the couple were allegedly killed that morning. He is accused of storing their bodies in surfboard bags in the courtyard before moving them.

The men’s bodies were found eight days later at Bungonia, south of Sydney. On Thursday, the court released photographs of Cella Farm in Bungonia, where police searched dams and farmland for evidence. The jury has heard Lamarre-Condon allegedly tried, unsuccessfully, to sink the bodies in a dam before returning and moving them.

The jury has also heard that DNA on parts of Lamarre-Condon’s police pistol, including the trigger, came from three people. Davies was matched as a major contributor and the accused as a minor contributor.

What The Crown And Defence Say

The Crown alleges Lamarre-Condon developed a jealous infatuation with Baird, a Channel Ten presenter he had been romantically involved with from 2022. It alleges Davies was shot through a bedroom door as he tried to call Triple Zero (000).

The defence case is that Davies pulled the trigger and fatally shot himself during a second struggle over the pistol. Lamarre-Condon’s lawyers have indicated he panicked after the deaths and cleaned the scene because he knew how it would look.

The three-month trial before Justice Natalie Adams continues.

Adelaide Film Festival Has Nine LGBTQIA+ Films In 2026

By Marvin Valdez
Posted on October 8, 2026
Jim Queen (Bobbypills), Rami Malek in The Man I Love. (Big Creek Projects), and Coward. (The Reunion)
Share
Tweet
Share
Share
Email

Adelaide Film Festival runs from 14 to 25 October, screening 122 films from 52 countries. Nine of them are LGBTQIA+ features. The lineup includes a French animated comedy about a Paris gym influencer, Lukas Dhont’s First World War drama Coward and new work from Ira Sachs. Here’s the full list, with trailers.

Jim Queen (France)

What happens when roided-up social media influencer, Jim Parfait, who rules Paris’s queer clubs with his bulging pecs, is struck down by the heinously beige plague heterosis? It’s left to nerdy twink Lucien to save the day, if only he can keep his right-wing mum at bay.

Coward (Belgium)

All queer on the Western Front, Emmanuel Macchia and Valentin Campagne are electric in this sensuously shot, achingly tender World War 1 drama from Close (2022) director Lukas Dhont. Rightly sharing the Best Actor award at Cannes, you’ll fall in step with these beautiful, longing soldiers.

Flesh And Fuel (France/Poland)

Hit the road with hunky truckers whose hot and heavy hook-up in a roadside beat leads to unexpected depths. Pierre Le Gall’s haunting debut feature is a luminous insight into life, lust and love all played out in a cramped truck cabin. Winner of the inaugural Queer Palm Revelation award at Cannes.

The Man I Love (USA)

Against the backdrop of the AIDS crisis, a theatre artist refuses to surrender to fear, finding solace in art, intimacy and community. Ira Sachs’ deeply felt drama is a moving ode to those who kept loving, creating and dreaming in the face of unimaginable loss.

Lovers In The Blue Night (India)

Married through pity, Karim and Ameena eke out a passionless life in Mumbai. She clings to anonymous letters, while he’s quietly gay. When Karim finds desire in a thief named Sukka, and Ameena mistakes a pimp for her letter-writer, four lonely lives collide in longing, illusion and survival.

Fruit Gathering (Myanmar)

A deep bond with unpredictable undercurrents forms between textile workers San Kyi and Theint Theint Oo. Is there a chance for something deeper between them or is the inexorable pull downwards inescapable in a Myanmar torn apart by brutal violence? Aung Phyoe’s heady drama dances between doom and desire.

The Way You See Me (Argentina/Mexico)

Filmed across a decade, this evolving self-portrait captures gender transition from the inside out, tracing a radical transformation from hyperfemininity towards defiant masculinity while confronting the forces of class, privilege and patriarchy within Mexico’s conservative upper-middle-class society.

Wolf (UK)

British avant-pop musician Patrick Wolf revisits the triumphs and traumas that shaped his life and music through a lifetime of archival material and a searching conversation with longtime friend Tilda Swinton. Delicate yet emotionally raw, Wolf exchanges mythmaking for vulnerability.

Sweet Milk Lake (Australia)

When the recently transitioned Jake heads to the rural town Sweet Milk to reconnect with his dying father, he is mistaken for his cis twin brother, Sam (writer-director Harvey Zielinski). Both privileges and a chasm of doubt open, enveloping Jake and those he loves in this emotionally rich tribute to living your truth.
